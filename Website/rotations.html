Rotation Overview:

ORTSOC follows a hospital clinical rotation format with rotations occurring every five weeks. Each rotation is meant to equip students with a myriad of Cybersecurity skills they could use in their future jobs. ORTSOC has multiple rotations so that students can experience different forms of Cybersecurity and see which type suits their future career best.
To the right side of this page, is a list and overview of each rotation.

Cybersecurity Assessment:

The Cybersecurity Assessment rotation emphasizes learning and practicing Cybersecurity assessments which assist clients in improving their security posture. Working with an ORTSOC client, your team will select a framework appropriate to the client's Cybersecurity goals and conduct an assessment. Our guide information on Cybersecurity Assessment is in the first chapter of the guide:

Network & Security Monitoring:

In the Network Security Monitoring and Incident Response rotation, you will monitor, analyze, investigate, and report on security events and incidents. The severity of an event, whether it is a security event or a security incident, will be determined by you and your peers conducting the investigation. Our guide information on Network & Security Monitoring is in the second chapter of the guide:

Beginner's Guide to ORTSOC/Chap2

Security Engineering:

In the Security Engineering rotation, you will be focusing on applying security principles to support and strengthening ORTSOC. You will gain hands-on experience designing, implementing, and maintaining both virtual and physical infrastructures that enable secure monitoring of client systems. Our guide information on Security Engineering is in the third chapter of the guide:

Beginner's Guide to ORTSOC/Chap3

Penetration Testing:

Penetration testers use many similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTP) as malicious attackers to find and demonstrate the business impact a weakness may result in. You will learn about and use these TTPs to conduct a penetration test. You will be expected to conduct penetration testing operations against client infrastructure. Our guide information on Penetration Testing is in the fourth chapter of the guide:

Beginner's Guide to ORTSOC/Chap4

Threat Intelligence:

Threat Intelligence is evidence-based knowledge about current or emerging Cybersecurity threats. It enables students to better understand potential threats. Throughout this rotation you will engage with real-world scenarios and current threat landscapes to develop practical skills in threat profiling and vulnerability assessment.
